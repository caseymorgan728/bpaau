1. Market overview

The Australian online gambling market is one of the largest per-capita gambling markets in the world. Australians have long had a strong affinity for gambling — particularly electronic gaming machines (pokies) — and the shift to online platforms has accelerated since 2020.

Under the Interactive Gambling Act 2001 (Cth), it is illegal for online casino operators to offer their services to Australian residents from within Australia. As a result, virtually all online casinos accessible to Australian players are operated offshore — predominantly under Gaming Curacao licences — and target Australian players from overseas jurisdictions. This regulatory structure means that Australian players access these sites on a legal grey-market basis, and the Australian government does not publish detailed statistics on online casino/pokies activity specifically.

Sources: Interactive Gambling Act 2001 (Cth); Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) online gambling enforcement reports; industry analyses by H2 Gambling Capital and Global Betting and Gaming Consultants.

Market size estimates

While exact online-pokies-only figures are not published by Australian regulators, industry analysts estimate the total Australian online gambling market (including sports betting, race betting, and online casino/pokies) at approximately AUD 30–35 billion in total wagering turnover in 2025. Online casino and pokies are estimated to account for a growing share of this total, driven by increased smartphone penetration and the expansion of offshore operators targeting the Australian market.

3. Payment method trends: the rise of PayID

PayID — Australia's instant payment addressing system, operated by NPP Australia — has become the dominant deposit and withdrawal method for Australian players at offshore online casinos. Its growth has been driven by three factors: (1) instant settlement (typically under 1 minute for deposits), (2) no chargeback risk for operators (unlike credit cards), and (3) widespread adoption by Australian banks (over 13 million registered PayID IDs as of 2025).

6. Responsible gambling data

Responsible gambling is a critical consideration in the Australian online pokies market. The following data draws on Australian government statistics and BPAAU's assessment of operator responsible gambling tools:

  • Problem gambling prevalence: Australian gambling prevalence studies estimate that approximately 0.5–1% of Australian adults experience severe problem gambling, with a further 2–3% experiencing moderate-risk gambling. Online casino/pokies players have a slightly higher prevalence of problem gambling compared to the general gambling population, consistent with international research.
  • Responsible gambling tool availability: Based on BPAAU's assessment of 20+ operators, approximately 85% offer self-exclusion, 75% offer deposit limits, 70% offer reality checks / session reminders, and 60% offer cool-off periods. However, the ease of accessing and using these tools varies significantly — some operators bury responsible gambling settings in account menus, while others promote them prominently.
  • Help-seeking: Gambling Help Online (gamblinghelponline.org.au, 1800 858 858) reports that online gambling-related contacts have increased steadily since 2020, consistent with the growth of online gambling participation.

Sources: Productivity Commission of Australia Inquiry into Gambling (2024); Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW) gambling statistics; Gambling Help Online service data; BPAAU responsible gambling tool assessment of 20+ operators.

7. Regulatory landscape

The Australian online gambling regulatory environment is defined by the Interactive Gambling Act 2001 (Cth), which prohibits the provision of interactive gambling services (including online casino games) to Australian residents by operators located in Australia. The Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) is responsible for enforcing the Act, including issuing notices to offshore operators and working with financial institutions and payment processors to block transactions to prohibited sites.

Despite these restrictions, Australian players can readily access offshore online casinos — predominantly operating under Gaming Curacao licences — because the operators are located outside Australia and the Act's extraterritorial reach is limited. The Australian government has signalled ongoing interest in strengthening enforcement, including potential reforms to the Interactive Gambling Act and increased resources for ACMA.

Players should be aware that accessing offshore online casinos from Australia exists in a legal grey area, and that consumer protections (such as dispute resolution and fund protection) that apply to licensed Australian gambling providers do not apply to offshore operators. BPAAU recommends that players only use operators that hold verifiable licences, offer responsible gambling tools, and have a track record of timely payouts.

Sources: Interactive Gambling Act 2001 (Cth); ACMA online gambling enforcement reports; Australian Government Department of Social Services gambling reform discussion papers.

8. Methodology and data limitations

This report combines BPAAU's independently collected data with publicly available sources. Key limitations:

  • Market size figures are estimates. Australian regulators do not publish online-pokies-specific turnover data due to the offshore nature of the market. Figures cited are drawn from industry analyst estimates and should be treated as indicative.
  • Operator sample. BPAAU's testing covers 20+ operators that target the Australian market and hold Gaming Curacao licences. This is not a census of all operators, and results may not generalise to operators outside our sample.
  • RTP data. RTP figures are as published by game providers. Some operators may offer reduced-RTP versions of certain games, which would not be captured in provider-published figures.
  • Self-reported data. Some demographic and prevalence figures are drawn from self-reported survey data, which may be subject to recall bias and underreporting of stigmatised behaviour.
  • Time period. BPAAU-collected data covers January 2025 to August 2026. The online gambling market evolves rapidly, and figures may change.

9. Conclusions and outlook

The Australian online pokies market in 2026 is characterised by continued growth in offshore operator participation, the dominance of PayID as the primary payment method, and increasing competition on no-deposit bonus offerings. From a player perspective, the key positive trends are faster payouts (median 2–4 hours via PayID), a wide selection of high-RTP games, and growing availability of responsible gambling tools. The key risks remain the legal grey-market status of offshore operators, the potential for bonus terms to be tightened, and the ongoing risk of problem gambling.
